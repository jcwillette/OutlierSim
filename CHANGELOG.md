- **Bot defense plays goal-side, and scorers slip round a defender** (ADR 0010). The defense role looked aimless and barely slowed anyone. Against three 2023 elevators, the shutdown setup (two elevators and an aggressive defender) took 0.5 ± 0.7 points off red over 240 paired matches. Instrumented, the defender spent 9 % of teleop defending and 71 % fetching and placing hybrid pieces. Now it takes 6.6 ± 0.8 points and 1.9 pieces a match (8.9 at hard difficulty, was 3.4). Blue's win rate goes from 8.8 % to 10.4 %; the defender's own scoring (4.9 → 2.4 points) gives back part of the gain.
  - With nobody to mark, the defender waits at the opponents' **gate**, on their carrying lane just outside the protected zones at their goal end (new `guard` plan; the HUD says "Waiting for a carrier in the opponents' path"). It no longer wanders off to score.
  - It marks the carrier it can get in front of soonest and stays on it. It meets the carrier on that carrier's line home, steps out to intercept within 3.5 m (swept 2.5-7 m), then drives into it from goal-side. From beside or behind it swings round the side instead of pushing the carrier toward its goal. Empty robots leaving the defender's area are worth 0.4 of a carrier.
  - It breaks off a pin by the referee's visible count (`Match.pinCounts`) instead of after 3 s of any slow contact. Holding a robot up in the open isn't a pin.
  - It won't push an opponent into the defender's own protected zones. The first version did, and drew about 40 points of red G207 fouls a match. Its corners stay out of the opponent's zones too (half-diagonal, not half-width). Foul rates stay even: red 0.8 → 1.3 G207 calls a match, blue 0.6 → 0.7, no pins.
  - **Counter-defense**: a bot with an opponent squarely in its way slips round it after a reaction time (`SKILL.react`: easy 0.6 s, normal 0.35 s, hard 0.2 s), never toward the opponent's protected zones or a wall. Without a defender this changes nothing measurable (0.0 ± 1.3 points over 120 paired 2023 matches).
  - **2024**: the defender predicts where a shooter will shoot from (`scoringSpot`: its nearest shot spot, from its robot type's range) and waits there. It reads the declared fouls rather than the bots' path zones (`MatchAI.fouling`). 2024 keeps paths out of the STAGE ZONE all match, which had made red's whole shooting area off limits to defense, though contact there is legal until the last 20 s. It lets go of opponents whose bumpers are, or are about to be, in zones where either robot counts (G423 / G424 / G422), gets out of endgame-only zones 5 s early, and keeps out of its own zones. For all-rounder, all-rounder and an aggressive defender against three all-rounders (240 paired matches), red's teleop falls by 4.3 points (10 %; the old defender: about 1), blue's margin gains 4.2 and its win rate goes from 5.8 % to 9.6 %. Blue's G424 calls go from 0.08 to 0.20 a match, mostly from a broken-down defender left in red's STAGE area.
  - **Model change** (ADR 0006): matches with a defense-role robot, or with opponents meeting head-on, change. ms per match is unchanged (620 vs 624). Random 2023 alliances include the defender 43 % of the time, and their average over the same 400 seeds moves from 87.9 to 84.2 points (teleop 43.3 → 40.9, fouls 3.3 → 2.0). The real 2023 average is 84.8. The calibration's teleop pieces were fitted with a defender that didn't slow anyone, so they now run about 6 % low. `npm run calibrate` wasn't rerun, because that needs the owner's request (ADR 0004).

  - **Declared fouls** (ADR 0008): `GameDefinition.fouls.contact` (protected-zone contact, e.g. G207) and `fouls.pinning` (e.g. G202), called every tick by the new referee (`src/engine/sim/referee.ts`).
    - Contact is bumper to bumper (footprint boxes within 3 cm). A zone counts any part of the robot unless `fully`, with an exact footprint-and-zone test (`footprintOverlaps` / `footprintInside` in `math.ts`, so a zone corner poking into a robot's side counts; the auto leave award and the audit's start-pose check use it too).     - A pin needs the pressed robot up against a wall, a field element or another robot beyond it, so a head-on shoving match isn't called on both robots and a pinned robot pushing back isn't called at all (as the first version did). It ends as the manual words it: the robots 6 ft apart, either 6 ft from where it started, or the pinning robot pinned by a third robot; pinning again within 3 s (`repinSec`) resumes the count.
