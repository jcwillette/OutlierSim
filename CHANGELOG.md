# Changelog

Format: newest first. One entry per merged PR.

## Unreleased

- Base-system pass after the 2023 stress test: what 2023 had to hand-write or work around is now part of the engine, the tools and the skill, so the next game declares it as data.
  - **Declared fouls** (ADR 0008): `GameDefinition.fouls.contact` (protected-zone contact, e.g. G207) and `fouls.pinning` (e.g. G202), called every tick by the new referee (`src/engine/sim/referee.ts`).
    - Contact is bumper to bumper (footprint boxes within 3 cm). A zone counts any part of the robot unless `fully`, with an exact footprint-and-zone test (`footprintOverlaps` / `footprintInside` in `math.ts`, so a zone corner poking into a robot's side counts; the auto leave award and the audit's start-pose check use it too). That calls about 5 % more G207 fouls in defended 2023 matches (734 → 768 over 120 Shutdown vs Typical matches, the same seeds); the 12 bench matches don't change. `forgiveForced` keeps the first build's lenient G201 approximation as an option.
    - A pin needs the pressed robot up against a wall, a field element or another robot beyond it, so a head-on shoving match isn't called on both robots and a pinned robot pushing back isn't called at all (as the first version did). It ends as the manual words it: the robots 6 ft apart, either 6 ft from where it started, or the pinning robot pinned by a third robot; pinning again within 3 s (`repinSec`) resumes the count.
    - Bots take their protected zones from these rules by default, keep their whole body (not just their center) out of the opponent's protected zones, give opponents extra room while partly inside one, and back off past the pin release gap.
  - **2023 uses them** (model change): G207 now applies to "any part" of a robot in the opponent's loading zone or community, as the manual says (p.58), with real bumper contact instead of centers within 0.95 m. Over 150 random-lineup seeds, G207 fouls per alliance per match went 0.6 → 1.6 and teleop points didn't move (74.6 / 72.6 → 73.4 / 73.7). Against a defender the scoring alliance draws ~4 G207 fouls per match, mostly pushed partly into the defender's own zone, which G201's blue box says is still a foul. `rules.ts` keeps only links, supercharge and the Charge Station level logic, and re-reads the grids only after a score.
  - **Ramps robots can climb**: box robots stalled at the base of the Charge Station ramp unless they hit it fast (every robot type from a slow approach; the balancer and the defender even at full speed from the start). Hulls with `nav: 'ramp'` now continue their floor edges 3 cm below the floor in physics, so robots drive up at any speed. The 2023 Charge Station is back at the manual's 9⅛ in (it was lowered to 0.18 m only for the bots' navigation). Docking is more reliable, so the Activation RP rate went up too (Typical alliance at normal: 81-90 % in the new runs; see SOURCES.md).
  - **Navigation hints**: `FieldElement.nav` (`'auto' | 'wall' | 'ramp' | 'none'`) overrides the height rule, so geometry can stay true to the manual.
  - **Pick the level to place on**: where several targets share a spot (2023's three rows at a column), V (gamepad D-pad ← →) switches which one Space places on, and the HUD shows it. It used to always be the highest-value row, so students couldn't place a mid node to finish a link.
  - **The HUD warns about contact fouls and pins**: in an opponent's protected zone (or next to an opponent protected in its own), the hint says touching them is a foul and names the rule (`Match.contactWarnings`, from the declared `fouls.contact`), so students learn the zones while they drive. While a student pins an opponent (or is pinned), the hint shows the referee's count ("Pinning Red 1: 3 s (a foul every 5 s). Back off 6 ft to end it.", `Match.pinCounts`).
  - **Fouls by rule on the results screen**: the match tallies fouls per alliance by reason (`AllianceSummary.foulCalls`), and the results list them ("Red: G207 contact in the opponent's loading zone or community ×4"), so students see which rules cost points. Pins are tallied as one rule (`G202 pinning`); the announcement keeps the duration.
  - **Ranking points**: new kind `pointsAtLeast` (points from listed ledger sources and categories); 2023's Activation RP uses it instead of a custom function.
  - **Endgame attempts can fail**: `RobotArchetype.endgameSuccess` (per action, default 100 %) and `EndgameAction.fallback` (2023: a failed engage still docks). Bots weigh it by expected points, and the menu's robot tuning has a success chance per endgame action. Defaults are unchanged; it answers the 2023 open question about balance failures as a knob.
  - **Choose the piece at a human player station**: `HumanPlayerStation.choice` lets the robot ask for one of the station's piece types (`RobotCommand.pieceType`). Players switch with F (gamepad X) and the HUD shows what they'll get; bots weigh each type and ask for the one they plan to score. Stations without it behave as before (bit-identical). The reference game's feeder offers blocks or orbs. 2023 keeps one shelf per piece type, from before this existed.
  - **Teammates make room**: a bot standing on the spot a higher-priority teammate announced (an endgame zone, a scoring spot) steps out of that teammate's lane, which breaks swap deadlocks where two teammates sit on each other's goals (reference game, 24 seeds: climbs per alliance 1.00 → 1.15). A second 2,000-match mirrored Powerhouse run after this: wins 1003 : 943 (z 1.4), non-foul score 180.3 vs 180.3.
  - **The reference game** (the engine's feature test) now declares a protected loading zone, a pin rule, `nav: 'ramp'` on its center ramp, a choice feeder and three lineup presets.
  - **Authoring helpers** (`src/engine/sim/authoring.ts`): `IN`, `FT`, `LB`, `DEG`, `feet()`, `rect()`, `poly()`, `circle()`, `cite()`.
  - **Bots' geometry audit** (`auditDefinition`, `tests/audit.test.ts` for every game): every place target, shot, station, endgame zone, park zone and staged piece must be usable by a robot type that has the mechanism, from the start poses. It names what the bots can't use and why.
  - **`npm run plausibility -- --game <id>`**: validation, the audit, a piece ceiling (preloads, both alliances' staged pieces, the alliance's human player supply, against target capacity), and every lineup preset against itself at easy / normal / hard in worker threads, printed as the SOURCES.md table with PASS/WARN checks (difficulty ordering, bonus-RP rates, unused targets and endgame actions, robot types that never score, blue vs red). 2023: 336 matches in about 2 minutes on 4 cores. `--against <lineup>` plays every preset against one lineup instead and prints the head-to-head table.
  - **Manual ingest** writes `rules.md` (every G/H/R/S/E/T rule with its page and first line) and `meta.txt` (the PDF's SHA-256 and dates) next to the text, the topic index and the figures. Both ingest scripts check for their tools and the input file first and say what to install.
  - **Validation** covers the new fields plus RP sources, tags and target ids, and `botHints` zone ids.
  - **Skill**: the extraction checklist asks for the manual's revision and SHA-256, the quoted G-rules, nav hints, RP kinds, reliability and lineups; the cookbook maps declared fouls, non-foul penalties, ramps, auto limits and rules state; SKILL.md has the audit and plausibility tool in Step 4, a step-by-step kickoff fast path and the pitfalls the 2023 build hit. The template uses the helpers and shows the new declarations.
  - Symmetry re-checked after the bot changes: 2,000 mirrored Powerhouse matches, wins 998 : 966 (z 0.7), score 185.2 ± 13.6 vs 185.1 ± 13.1.
- `npm run sim -- --lineups a,b` with two different lineups prints each alliance's stats and RP rates separately (the pooled table mixed the two setups); the JSON has `bySide`.
- Faster, same results (ADR 0006; each step verified with `npm run bench --compare`). Net of the new per-tick work this pass adds (the referee, protected-zone awareness, making room), a 2023 match takes about as long as on `main` (844 → 857 ms, min of 3 runs of 8 seeds on 4 cores) and a reference match ~5 % longer (it now exercises more features); without these speed-ups both would be ~10-15 % slower:
  - Nav grids build about 9× faster (2023: 44 → 5 ms per grid, six per match start), which also removes a hitch when a match starts in the browser.
  - Bots look up their nav grid once per robot, collect protected zones and path penalties once per match, and cache endgame spot data; touch tracking skips far pairs exactly; aperture checks skip place-only games; the per-tick shot check tests one target.
  - About 4 % (2023) to 9 % (reference) per match; physics is now ~45-58 % of a match.
  - The protected-zone checks the bots run every tick use a bounding-box shortcut and cached zone outlines, and the referee works out each robot's heading trig once per tick: another 7-8 % per match.

- Blue and red now play the same game (ADR 0007):
  - Identical 2023 alliances used to give red a wider score spread and fewer bonus RPs than blue. In 2,500 matches: score SD 13.8 vs 12.3, Sustainability 98.4 % vs 99.4 %. Auto showed it most: red's node count varied more, and blue missed Mobility 3× as often.
  - Cause 1, navigation: the bots' 0.12 m nav grid started at the blue corner, so red's half was 1.8 cm off-grid, and A* broke ties in fixed directions. Red's bots drove a slightly different maze. Now each alliance plans on a grid laid out from its own wall (`AllianceNav`). Fixed left/right preferences (approach headings, which way to step aside) flip for red on a mirrored field.
  - Cause 2, float32 rounding: Rapier's origin was the blue corner, so red's half was rounded about 8× more coarsely. Rapier's world is now centered on the field.
  - Cause 3, Rapier's contact solver: it resolves a contact a little differently from its mirror image, which favored one end. Rapier's world is now flipped on even seeds (`PhysicsFrame`, `MatchOptions.flipPhysics`), so consecutive seeds alternate ends and a run favors neither.
  - Half-turn ties now break the same way for both alliances (`turnTo`). At spawn on the rotational reference game, blue and red used to turn opposite ways.
  - Result, same setup and seeds (2,500 matches), blue vs red: wins 48.9 % vs 48.1 % (was 47.3 % vs 50.5 %), score 182.4 ± 12.3 vs 181.8 ± 12.2 (was ± 12.3 vs ± 13.8), Sustainability 99.5 % vs 99.5 % (was 99.4 % vs 98.4 %), Activation 80.6 % vs 81.2 % (was 81.5 % vs 79.5 %), Mobility 8.92 vs 8.92 points. In 5,000 auto-only matches, auto points are 54.6 vs 54.7 and Mobility misses 98 vs 91 (was 68 vs 21 per 2,000). Matches are no slower (783 vs 815 ms).
  - Every match changes (a model change, not a speed-up); the same seed still replays exactly. `Robot.setPosition` / `setVelocity` and `Piece.setPosition` / `setVelocity` take field coordinates; raw `body` calls see Rapier's frame (`match.physics`).
  - New `tests/symmetry.test.ts`: red's nav answers are exact images of blue's for every game, and with fixed randomness red's bots mirror blue's decisions and moves. It fails on the old code. The teammate-contact test now averages 8 matches instead of 2 (per-match contact ranges from 2 to 22 s).
- Faster win probability, same results:
  - Each headless match is about 2× faster, and the same seed still plays the exact same match, bit for bit (ADR 0006). Per match, min of 3 runs: 2023 random lineups 1.45 → 0.77 s, 2023 presets 1.28 → 0.63 s, reference game 1.34 → 0.90 s.
    - Robot and piece positions are cached per physics step instead of being read from Rapier each time (about 4 million reads per 2023 match). Any change to a body clears the cache.
    - The physics step skips Rapier 0.21's soft-body bookkeeping, which walked every body and collider after each step.
    - A* reuses its buffers, keeps its heap in typed arrays and precomputes the wall tests. On 2,300 recorded 2023 searches it is 2.5× faster and returns identical paths.
    - Nav grids and shot spots are built once per game instead of once per match. The nearest shot spot is found with a bucket index.
    - 2023 rules snapshot the robots and GRIDs once per tick.
  - One worker per logical core, up to 16 (was cores − 1, at most 8). In Chromium on 4 cores, 4 workers did 19 % more match-ups per second than 3.
  - Together: a 1000-match-up 2023 run on a 4-core machine dropped from 7.3 min to about 2.7 min. The unit tests run in 16 s instead of 23 s.
  - New `npm run bench`: time per match plus a fingerprint of every match; `--compare before.json` fails if any match changed. New `tests/speed.test.ts` pins every speed-up to the plain code it replaced. `?threads=N` overrides the worker count.
- Win probability (Monte Carlo) in spectator mode:
  - The watch menu has a **Win probability** section: pick 100-2,500 match-ups and press **Simulate match-ups**. Each match-up plays the menu's exact setup (robots, roles, strategies, bot skill per alliance, breakdowns, tuning) bot vs bot, headless; Random slots are re-dealt every match-up.
  - The results screen updates live: each alliance's win chance with a 95 % Wilson interval, ties, mean / median / 10th-90th percentile scores, average RP and each bonus RP rate, and a histogram of winning margins. **Stop** keeps what has run so far; **Run again** uses fresh seeds.
  - **Watch a typical match** and **Watch an upset** replay a sampled match-up exactly (same seed, same robots dealt, same score).
  - Matches run in parallel Web Workers (`src/ui/montecarlo.worker.ts`, logical cores − 1, at most 8), pulling seeds from a shared queue. Without Worker support they run on the main thread. ADR 0005.
  - `src/ui/montecarlo.ts` (DOM-free, tested) has `runMatchup`, `summarize`, `wilson`, `pickReplays`. `npm run sim` now prints win rates too.
- Drive speed, acceleration and weight from real hardware:
  - New `src/engine/sim/drivetrain.ts`. It holds vendor motor specs (CIM, NEO, NEO Vortex, Falcon 500, Kraken X60 with and without FOC) and common gearings (SDS MK4i L1-L3, REV MAXSwerve 12-14T, Kit of Parts tank ratios). `driveFor(swerve(...) | tank(...), maxOmega)` turns them into `maxSpeed` and `maxAccel`: practical top speed is 85 % of free speed for swerve and 77 % for tank, and acceleration comes from the motor curve with typical drive current limits, traction and an optional tipping limit, fitted to a 5 m sprint. `onFieldMass(lb)` adds ~13 lb of battery and ~14 lb of bumpers.
  - 2023 robots now state their hardware. Top speeds are 10-15.5 ft/s, accelerations 23-44 ft/s², and on-field weights 117-155 lb (previously 143 lb or less). The full table is in `2023-chargedup/SOURCES.md`. Bot scoring barely moves (normal, random lineups, 40 matches: 182 → 178 points per alliance).
  - The reference game and the template use the helper too. The skill and the extraction checklist now ask for each robot's drivetrain and weight instead of bare m/s numbers.
- Robot & field tuning in the menu (collapsible, saved per game):
  - Per robot type: max speed (ft/s), acceleration (ft/s²), turn rate (°/s), wheel grip, weight (lb), pickup time, place time per target, shooter reload / spread / range (ft), each endgame action's time, and failure chances (breakdown, mechanism failure, each one's recovery chance, power losses per match). Blank = the game's default, shown as the placeholder. Applies to every robot of that type, yours and the bots'.
  - Per human player station: hand-off time, time between pieces, pieces per alliance.
  - `src/ui/tuning.ts` (DOM-free, tested) holds the fields and `applyTuning`, which returns a tuned copy of the definition. `npm run sim` takes the same overrides with `--tuning file.json`.
  - New `RobotArchetype.faults` overrides the fault rates derived from `reliability` (`faultRates()` in `src/engine/sim/faults.ts`).
- Partial failures and recovery:
  - New mechanism failure: the robot still drives (and can still do endgame actions) but can't intake, place, shoot or use a human player station. Chance about 1.5 × (1 − R) per match, 2.5× a breakdown. Tagged NO MECH; bots with a broken mechanism play defense, then go to the endgame.
  - Breakdowns are no longer always permanent. A broken-down robot comes back after 10-35 s with chance 0.7 − 3 × (1 − R); a failed mechanism comes back after 5-25 s with chance 0.85 − 3 × (1 − R) (both clamped to 5-95%). Better robots come back more often.
  - Results show when a robot came back or its mechanism was fixed. `npm run sim` reports mechanism-failure and recovery rates.
- Clean defense:
  - Defenders never mark an opposing defender. Defender-vs-defender time when both alliances bring one: ~136 s → ~0 s per match. A Defender with no worthwhile target scores instead.
  - They drop an opponent within 0.6 m of its protected zones and never wait inside them.
  - They break off after 3 s pressed against a stopped opponent, then leave that robot alone for 4 s.
  - New `botHints.endgameProtectedZones` (G209-style).
  - Fouls by a defending alliance roughly halved (2023 Shutdown, 40 matches: 1.02 → 0.53 per match). The defender's effect on the opponent is unchanged (about 3 teleop points).
- `RobotInfo` has the commanded drive direction (`cmdX`, `cmdY`) for contact rules.
- 2023: G202 pinning is enforced (FOUL at 5 s, TECH FOUL every further 5 s). Added an invisible Charge Station zone for G209.
- Imperfect robots:
  - `RobotArchetype.reliability` (0..1, default 0.95). With `MatchOptions.breakdowns`, a robot can break down for the rest of the match (about 0.6 × (1 − R)) or lose power / comms for 2-6 s (about 5 × (1 − R) per match). Its action is interrupted, it is drawn gray with a DISABLED / NO POWER tag, and the HUD announces it.
  - Bots have tunnel-vision lapses where they ignore teammates' calls and right of way. These are more frequent for easy bots and low-reliability robots.
  - The app's Breakdowns setting can be off, bots only (the default) or everyone. `npm run sim` has breakdowns on unless you pass `--perfect`, and it reports breakdown rates.

- Bot teamwork:
  - Alliance bots share where they're headed and avoid spots a teammate has called, including the node next door.
  - They plan paths around stopped robots.
  - In a lane conflict, the lower-priority teammate steps aside (priority: docking, then carrying a piece, then robot number).
  - Teammate contact in 2023 dropped from ~27 s to ~7 s per match. Reference game scores rose (normal ~650 → ~715) because fewer cycles are lost to collisions.
- Opportunistic defense: an empty-handed bot near an opponent carrying a piece home may block it for up to 4 s, when that costs the opponent more than the bot would score (`botHints.defenseOpportunism`, default 1, 0 disables). Low-scoring robots defend most.
- `botHints.protectedZones`: bots don't defend inside, or chase loose pieces into, the opponent's protected zones, and they path around them when that's not much longer.
- Navigation: ramps and platforms (drivable elements 0.05-0.2 m tall) cost 3× in pathfinding, and bots drive at most 1.5 m/s on them. Bots stopped launching off the far side of the Charge Station (hops in 90 matches: 145 → 21).
- 2023 Charged Up: G207 right of way is enforced. Contact while in the opponent's loading zone or community is a 5-point foul on the intruder.
